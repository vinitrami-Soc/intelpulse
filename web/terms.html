Terms of use

These terms cover the IntelPulse software and its public demo, and are meant to be read in one sitting. They are not legal advice. A server someone else runs is theirs, and they may set terms of their own.

1. The software

IntelPulse is free and open source under the MIT licence (opens GitHub in a new tab). You may use, copy, change and share it, commercially too, as long as the copyright and licence notice go with it. The licence text is what governs; this page only explains it.

2. The demo

  • The demo runs in your browser on synthetic data. It has no server and no accounts, and it sends nothing you paste anywhere.
  • It keeps your recent demo cases and lists in your browser's own storage, on your device, until you clear them. GitHub, which hosts the demo's files, may keep its own request logs under its own terms.
  • It is for trying IntelPulse. Its verdicts come from a bundled sample dataset, not from live intelligence, so do not act on them.

3. Using it fairly

  • Use IntelPulse on alerts, logs and indicators you are allowed to handle, to defend systems you are responsible for.
  • Do not use it to attack, scan or harass anyone, or to break the law where you or your server are.
  • Stay within the terms and the rate limits of every intelligence provider you connect.

4. Intelligence providers

IntelPulse can query AbuseIPDB, AlienVault OTX, GreyNoise, ThreatFox and URLhaus with API keys a workspace adds itself, and it reads offline datasets such as MaxMind GeoLite2, FireHOL, Feodo Tracker, CISA's catalogue of known exploited vulnerabilities and NVD. Each has terms of its own, and using a key or a dataset means accepting them. IntelPulse is not affiliated with, endorsed or sponsored by any of them; their names are used only to say what it connects to.

5. A verdict supports a decision

A score is a weighted composite of what the sources said, and a source can be wrong, late or missing. IntelPulse shows its working so that an analyst can check it. Review a verdict before you block, isolate or report anything on the strength of it, and treat a low-confidence verdict as provisional.

6. Running your own server

Whoever runs an IntelPulse server is responsible for it: who may sign in, the data its workspaces bring, its keys and backups, and the law it operates under. The privacy page says what the software itself stores and sends, and the deploy guide (opens GitHub in a new tab) how to run it safely.

7. No warranty

As the MIT licence says, IntelPulse is provided "as is", without warranty of any kind, and its authors are not liable for any claim, damages or other liability arising from it or its use. Keep your own backups, and try a change on a test server before relying on it.

8. Changes and questions

These terms can change with a release; the date below says when they last did, and every change is in the project's history. For a question about them, open an issue (opens GitHub in a new tab). Report a security issue privately, as the security policy (opens GitHub in a new tab) asks.
